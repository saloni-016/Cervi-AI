4. Causes and Risk Factors

  • Persistent infection with high-risk types of human papillomavirus (HPV) is the principal cause of nearly all cervical cancers.
  • Not attending recommended cervical screening can delay the detection of precancerous changes.
  • Smoking and certain conditions that weaken the immune system can increase the risk of cervical disease.
  • A cell image classification alone cannot identify the cause of an individual patient's findings.

5. Do's

  • Discuss the result with a qualified healthcare professional.
  • Follow age- and guideline-appropriate cervical screening recommendations.
  • Ask whether an HPV test, Pap test, repeat screening, or further evaluation is appropriate.
  • Keep track of screening appointments and follow-up recommendations.
  • Consider HPV vaccination according to local medical guidance if eligible.

6. Don'ts

  • Do not interpret the AI prediction or its confidence as a cancer diagnosis.
  • Do not treat the heatmap as proof of an affected or cancerous area.
  • Do not skip or delay professional assessment because the model reports a low category.
  • Do not start medication or treatment based solely on this report.
  • Do not assume that a normal-looking image rules out disease.
